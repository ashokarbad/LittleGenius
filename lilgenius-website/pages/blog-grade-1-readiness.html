Is my child ready for Grade 1?

Parents almost always ask this as an academic question. Grade 1 teachers, when you ask them, answer it as a self-management question. Both matter, and the second one is the half that gets neglected.

The academic half

By the end of Senior KG, most children are able to:

  • Recognise all letters and their sounds, and blend simple words
  • Read short, familiar sentences with some support
  • Write their own name and simple words without tracing
  • Count to 100, recognise numerals, and add and subtract small quantities
  • Identify shapes, colours, opposites and simple patterns
  • Speak in full sentences and describe something that happened yesterday

A child who is not yet fluent at reading is not behind. Reading fluency arrives across a wide age band, and pushing it earlier buys very little by Grade 3.

The half that decides how Grade 1 actually goes

Ask any Grade 1 teacher which children have a hard first term, and the answer is rarely about phonics. It is about these:

  • Managing themselves. Opening a tiffin, using the washroom independently, wiping, washing hands, drinking enough water, keeping track of a bag and a bottle.
  • Sustained attention. Staying with one task for fifteen to twenty minutes without an adult sitting alongside.
  • Following two-step instructions. “Put your book away and line up at the door” — heard once, done without prompting.
  • Asking for help. Being willing to tell an adult “I don’t understand” in a room of forty children.
  • Recovering from frustration. Getting something wrong, and continuing rather than shutting down.
  • Waiting. Tolerating not being first, not being chosen, and not being noticed for a whole hour.

What you can do in the last six months

Very little of it looks like academics. Stop doing the things your child can do slowly by themselves — shoes, buttons, packing the bag — even though it costs you ten minutes each morning. Read together daily and let them tell you what happened in the story. Give two-step instructions at home and wait rather than repeating.

If you are going to buy one thing for Grade 1 readiness, make it a tiffin your child can open alone. It sounds trivial. Ask any Grade 1 teacher how much of week one is spent opening lunch boxes.

About school interviews

Many Pune schools hold a short interaction for Grade 1 entry. In practice it is a conversation: name, family, colours, a picture to talk about, a rhyme. Children who have been rehearsed to answer in fixed sentences often do worse than children who chat comfortably, because the moment they get an unexpected question, the script fails. Prepare by talking to your child more, not by drilling answers.

If your child is not there yet

Being on the younger side of the cohort is genuinely disadvantageous in the first two years and largely evens out afterwards. If your child turns six well after school starts, and is struggling with attention and self-management rather than with letters, an extra year is a legitimate option and not a failure. Talk it through with the teacher who sees your child every day.
