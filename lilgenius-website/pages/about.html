About Li’l Genius

A small school that knows every child by name

Li’l Genius Pre-School opened in Balewadi in {{established}} with one classroom, a handful of children and a simple idea: young children learn best when they are known well.

We have grown since then, but deliberately slowly. We are still a single centre, still owner-run, and still small enough that the person who answers the phone knows which child is having a hard week and why.

Families come to us from Balewadi, Baner, Sus Road, Pashan and Wakad — mostly working parents who need a place that is genuinely good for their child and genuinely workable around a job.

Our vision

That every child who leaves Li’l Genius steps into Grade 1 curious rather than anxious — confident that questions are welcome, that mistakes are useful, and that they are capable of doing things for themselves.

Our mission

  • Keep groups small enough that no child is overlooked.
  • Teach through play, materials and conversation before pencil and paper.
  • Build honesty, kindness and responsibility into the daily routine, not into a poster.
  • Work as partners with parents, with nothing hidden.

Teaching philosophy

How we think about early learning

A two-year-old does not learn the concept of “three” by tracing it. They learn it by handing out three spoons, finding three stones, clapping three times, and being asked “how many now?” twenty times in a week. Our classrooms are set up for that kind of learning.

Play is the method, not the reward

Sand, water, dough, blocks, dress-up and role play are not what children do after the work is finished. For this age group they are the work. Formal writing is introduced only when a child’s hand and attention are ready, usually in Junior KG.

Independence over compliance

Children put on their own shoes, carry their own bag, pour their own water and clear their own plate — slowly and imperfectly at first. This is the practical meaning of our motto, “{{tagline}}”: do it yourself, and do it as well as you can.

Values are taught in the moment

Honesty, loyalty and compassion are learned when a four-year-old owns up to a broken toy, or comforts a friend who has been left out. We use those moments rather than lecture about them.

English with room for home language

The classroom runs in English, but Marathi and Hindi are never treated as a mistake. A child who is settling in is spoken to in whatever language helps them feel safe first.
