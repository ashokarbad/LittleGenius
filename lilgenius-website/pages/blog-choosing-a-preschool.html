Ten questions worth asking on a preschool tour

Every preschool tour in Pune shows you roughly the same things: bright walls, a play area, a wall of certificates. None of that tells you what your child’s Tuesday morning will feel like. These questions do.

1. How many children are in the room, and how many adults?

Ask for the number in the specific room your child would join, not the school average. A ratio of 1:10 means something quite different from 1:10 “including the helper and the principal”. If the answer is vague, ask to stand in the doorway and count.

2. How long has the class teacher been here?

Staff turnover is the most underrated signal in early education and the one nobody advertises. A school where teachers stay for years is almost always a school that treats its teachers well — and your two-year-old will not have to attach to a new adult every term.

3. What does the day actually look like, hour by hour?

A good answer is specific: arrival, circle time, a concept session, snack, activity, outdoor play. A weak answer is a list of adjectives. If forty minutes of the morning cannot be accounted for, it is usually screen time or waiting.

4. When do children start formal writing?

This is the question that most reliably splits preschools in two. A three-year-old pushed into handwriting produces neat books and a tired, discouraged hand. Pre-writing work — threading, tearing, clay, tweezers, big strokes — should come first, with letter formation arriving around Junior KG.

Be sceptical of a school that proudly shows you identical, beautifully finished artwork from a class of twenty three-year-olds. Somebody else finished it.

5. What happens when a child cries at drop-off in week three?

Everyone has an answer for week one. Week three is the real question, because it is when a school either investigates or starts saying “he is fine two minutes after you leave” on autopilot.

6. How and when will I hear from you?

Ask specifically: will I be called the same day if my child is hurt, however minor? Will I be told if she had a difficult morning, or only if there is good news? A school that only reports positives is managing you, not informing you.

7. Who can collect my child, and how do you verify it?

There should be an authorised pick-up list with photographs and a firm rule about one-off changes. If the answer is “we know all the parents”, that is a policy that works right up until the day it does not.

8. Can I see the washrooms?

Ask to see them, and to see where children eat. These two rooms are the honest ones. Reception areas are decorated for visitors; a washroom at 11 am is not.

9. What is your illness policy, and is it enforced?

A strict policy is inconvenient for you exactly once and saves your child several infections a year. A school that lets feverish children stay because parents have to work is not doing anyone a favour.

10. Can I speak to a parent from that class?

Any confident school will connect you. Five minutes with a current parent is worth more than an hour of anyone’s marketing, including ours.

Two more things, specific to Pune

First, visit in the monsoon if you can, or at least ask what happens to outdoor play in July. Schools with no usable indoor play space have a very long, very restless rainy season.

Second, work out the actual commute at 9 am on a weekday, not at 3 pm on a Sunday. A preschool ten minutes further away is fine; a school that turns into a forty-minute crawl through Baner traffic will wear down the whole family by October.
